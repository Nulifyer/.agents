# Visual departures

Read this when creating a new visual identity or page. Each example shows the arrangement a screen usually gets, one departure drawn from the subject's own content, and the cost to avoid. Take the reasoning, not the treatment. Do not copy a palette, typeface, or motif from these examples.

## Weather

- Usual: a large temperature with an icon, then a row of daily cards.
- Departure: organize around the person's day. A single timeline shows when rain starts and stops against the hours they will be out.
- Cost to avoid: keep the current temperature and conditions immediately readable.

## Recipe

- Usual: a hero photo, a long introduction, then ingredients and steps.
- Departure: organize around cooking. Ingredients scale with the serving count, and each step shows its quantities and timers where they are needed.
- Cost to avoid: do not hide the ingredient list people check before shopping.

## Developer documentation

- Usual: a navigation sidebar and paragraphs of explanation before any code.
- Departure: lead with a working example the reader can copy, then explain its parts.
- Cost to avoid: keep reference details complete and searchable below the example.

## Dashboard

- Usual: a row of summary tiles above a chart.
- Departure: lead with the one thing that needs attention, and show it against what was expected.
- Cost to avoid: do not drop the context people use to judge whether the rest is normal.

## Checking a departure

- It comes from the subject's content or the person's task, not from a general style.
- It carries information or context the usual arrangement lacks.
- Keep its surroundings disciplined. Repeat graphics only when comparison needs them; otherwise state each item's conclusion and show the graphic once or on demand.
- It does not move the task off the first screen at the narrowest width.
- It adds no filler text, fields, or sections.
